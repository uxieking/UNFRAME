What it is

A one-page interactive piece by LENSVRA. The viewer watches one moment through three emotional lenses (angry, sad, happy), says what mood they see after each clip, sees the quiet unfiltered clip, and then gets their answers handed back next to the real treatments. Near the end of each lens clip, a visual effect for that feeling comes in. When the viewer takes the test again, the effects are reversed.

index.htmlWhat is on the page: opening, clip and reveal screens
style.cssHow it looks: cinema dark, purple, white, grey, fades
script.jsWhat happens: texts and settings at the top, then the journey
effects.jsThe visual effects at the end of each lens clip
media/The four clips, with lens and music built in

Nothing is saved or sent anywhere. The answers live only in the page's memory.

The route: three stops

Toured together during the build wrap-up. Search for the quoted text to find each stop; line numbers are from commit 6d440cc and can drift.

  1. When should the effect start?
    script.js, search for "timeupdate" (around line 151)

    While a clip plays, the video tells the page how far it has come. The page works out how much is left and starts the effect once that is less than EFFECT_SECONDS. The normal clip is skipped, so it stays quiet.

    if (video.duration - video.currentTime <= EFFECT_SECONDS) {
  2. Forward or reversed?
    script.js, search for round % 2 (around line 154)

    round counts how many times the viewer pressed Start. Every second time, the remainder after dividing by 2 is 0, so the effect is reversed.

    const reversed = round % 2 === 0;
  3. Where the effect is drawn
    effects.js, search for function sad(reversed) (around line 291)

    Builds 18 drops: blue and purple falling, or gold rising when reversed. Further down, the comment // The one that goes the other way marks the single counter-drop. angry() and happy() sit right next to it and follow the same pattern.

How the rest of the journey works

startJourney() empties the answers and plays the first clip. playClip() fades between clips. When a lens clip ends, the question appears; recordAnswer() stores the answer or "skipped" and moves on. After the normal clip, Continue calls showReveal(), which builds the four small clips, the answer lines from answerLine(), and the closing question. restart() goes back to the opening screen.

A practice to reuse

Describe the feeling concretely, and the code can follow it closely. The end-of-clip effects were described as motion, colour and one counterweight per feeling (for example: blue and purple drops fall and make rings, one gold drop rises). That description went almost word for word into the plan (devpost/prd.md, Look and Feel), into the comments at the top of effects.js, and into one function per feeling. It was then checked in the browser and tried by the creator.

On another project, describing what someone should see happen (what moves, in which direction, what stands out) gives an agent something it can build and check, instead of a mood it has to guess.
